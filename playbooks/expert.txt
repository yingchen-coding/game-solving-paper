1. Before acting, write down what the evidence so far allows and what it rules out; act only inside what is still possible.
2. If an action gave the same result as before, your hypothesis is wrong: change the hypothesis, never repeat the action.
3. When a check fails after your fix, assume your fix is wrong before you assume the check is wrong.
4. Choose the action whose result splits the remaining possibilities most evenly, so every result teaches you something.
5. Compare expected with observed at an intermediate point to find where things first go wrong, then look only there.
6. Collect enough evidence to pin the answer down uniquely (count the unknowns) before committing to it.
7. Before you answer, check the answer against every observation you have; if any one disagrees, it is not the answer.
8. Prefer the smallest change that explains all failures over a rewrite.
